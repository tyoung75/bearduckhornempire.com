## This Dev Log Writes Itself Now

## Motus: Building Toward a Watch-First Experience

Motus had a big week. I shipped 21 commits focused on cardio tracking and workout reliability — the foundation for running and cycling on Apple Watch.

**Cardio workout support** is now functional: outdoor run, outdoor cycle, indoor run, and indoor cycle all work end-to-end with GPS tracking, heart rate monitoring, and workout summaries. Each activity type maps to the correct HealthKit workout type so data syncs properly to Apple Health and third-party apps.

I also added **hiking and walking support**. Hiking emphasizes elevation gain/loss in the UI. Walking focuses on step count and light movement. Both track GPS and heart rate.

**Strava auto-upload** shipped too. After a workout syncs to iPhone, it automatically uploads to Strava via their API if you've connected your account. Includes GPS route, heart rate data, and activity type. One less manual export step.

The big feature still in progress: **interval workout guidance**. For structured cardio (think: 6x800m repeats at 6:45 pace), the watch needs to show current interval, remaining time, and next interval preview. It should auto-advance between work and rest intervals with a countdown haptic. Pace alerts when you drift outside the target zone. This is the killer feature for runners who follow training plans. Not done yet, but the foundation is in place.

I also spent time on the watchOS app icon — designing for all required sizes (24x24 up to 1024x1024). The challenge: it has to be recognizable at tiny sizes on an OLED screen. Simple, bold mark. No gradients or fine details that vanish at 24 pixels.

## Infrastructure and Tooling Cleanup

I cleared out a bunch of technical debt this week.

The **task management system** got smarter. I expanded the database constraint on `tasks.source` to allow `manual`, `cowork`, and `api` alongside the old `linear_import` value. Linear is archived now — this reflects the new reality.

The **command bar** now handles natural language commands beyond just search and task creation. You can delete tasks, mark them done, snooze them, or reprioritize them — all via freeform text. I tried to delete a task the other day and nothing happened. That gap is fixed.

I also added a **delete/dismiss button** to the High-Leverage Actions cards. Before, you could only Snooze or mark Done. But some tasks become irrelevant and shouldn't pollute the wins log. Now you can dismiss them cleanly.

**Life Pillars got a UX upgrade**: clicking a goal (e.g., "Berlin Marathon sub-3:10") now navigates to a dedicated goal detail page showing progress tracking, related tasks, timeline, and linked content. This is the missing connective tissue between high-level goals and day-to-day work.

I also cleaned up the project issue board — deleted 42 duplicate and canceled issues to free up space under the free tier limit. Housekeeping, but necessary.
